The absurd bit

Not the right shape. Still the right dinner.

Every week, Latvian shops pull tonnes of food off sale for reasons that have nothing to do with whether it’s good. Wrong curve. Old logo. A dent in the box. A best-before printed with a letter O. We buy that food and sell it to you — cheap, with the reason on the label.

  • Grade A taste. Grade B mugshot.
  • The EU set a legal limit on how far a cucumber may bend. It set none on how good it tastes.
  • Same shop, a third off. That’s a month of dinners, back in your pocket.
  • An AI sorts every supermarket’s surplus overnight. That’s the whole business — and why it’s cheap.

      Produce

      Hail-marked, odd-shaped, perfectly good.

      Surprise-bag apps do a real job — but they hand you a sealed bag and hope for the best. Rescue is the opposite bet: a fixed address, named products, visible dates, and a reason printed on every label.

      Because you see what you’re buying, you can plan around it — dinner tonight, something for the freezer, staples for the cupboard. And because we’re a store you come back to, we do what an app can’t: member prices, a stamp card, a quiet first-pick hour before the after-work rush.

            The pipeline

            Everyone’s surplus, sorted by machine.

            Live intake · 38 stores connected · parcels move to Rescue as each reject list updates.

            Supermarkets already file a rejected-goods list every day — for audit, for write-off, for insurance. We plug into those feeds. A model reads every line, scores how sellable it is against our own health-and-hygiene rules, and routes it. People spot-check; the machine does the first pass on all of it.

            Concept figures. “Sky&More” and “Your Neighbour Grocery” stand in for the long tail of small chains and corner shops that a manual buyer could never work through.

            1. Ingest

              We read each partner’s daily reject list straight from their system: product, quantity, stated reason, batch, best-before, and the cold-storage temperature log.

              Live feeds: 38

            2. AI triage

              A model scores every line for sellability — shelf-life left, why it was pulled, storage history, recall status — and sorts roughly 1,900 lines a day in minutes.

              Avg score: 7.4 / 10

            3. Our standards

              Then the hard rules: cold chain never broken, a safe date margin, packaging intact, allergens labelled, nothing under recall.

              Pass rate: 71%

              → Fails go to compost, animal feed, or back to the store.

            4. On the shelf

              What passes is priced — about a third under retail, usually more — and listed on the shop tab, most of it within the hour.

              Median time in: 52 min
